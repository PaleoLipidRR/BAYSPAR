How a prediction is made

Six steps, the same in all three

Find the cellWhich 20° × 20° calibration cell holds the site. Choose draws1,000 of 20,000 posterior samples of α, β, τ². Set the priorMean of modern temperatures within 500 km. InvertA conjugate normal update for every draw. SampleOne temperature per TEX₈₆ value per draw. Summarise5th, 50th and 95th percentiles.

Analogue mode replaces the first step with a search for cells whose modern TEX₈₆ looks like the sample. The forward model runs the calibration the other way, from temperature to TEX₈₆.

bayspar_tex

bayspar_tex.mbaysparpy/bayspar_tex.pybayspar/predict.py · predict_seatemp

Turns a TEX₈₆ series from one core into temperatures. The calibration's slope and intercept come from the grid cell that contains the core, which assumes the ocean there behaved as it does today.

The math

A regression, inverted one draw at a time

Calibration, per cellTEX86 = α + β T + ε,   ε ~ N(0, τ²)
Prior on temperatureT ~ N(μ₀, σ₀²)
Posterior precisionλ = 1/σ₀² + β²/τ²
Posterior meanμ = μ₀/σ₀² + β (x − α)/τ²λ,   sd = 1/√λ

One TEX₈₆ value, one draw

The likelihood of a TEX₈₆ value x, read as a function of temperature, is a normal curve centred on (x − α)/β with width τ/β. The posterior is the precision-weighted compromise between it and the prior. Parameters here are the median draw for this site's cell; the prior mean is the one all three implementations compute for it.

priorlikelihoodposterior

Where the prior mean comes from

Each dot is a 1° cell of modern sea-surface temperature. The prior mean is the average of every cell within 500 km of the core (filled), here of them. The dashed square is the edge of the 20° calibration cell centred on (10° W, 0°) whose α and β the site uses.

bayspar_tex_analog

bayspar_tex_analog.mbaysparpy/bayspar_tex_analog.pybayspar/predict.py · predict_seatemp_analog

For deep time, where a core's modern position says little about its past ocean. Instead of one cell, it pools every calibration cell whose modern coretops have TEX₈₆ like the sample's, then runs the same inversion as standard mode across all of them.

The selection

Which cells count as analogues

Mean coretop TEX₈₆ in each of the 80 cells

Wilson Lake's 52 samples average . With search_tol = (twice their standard deviation), cells fall inside the window. All three implementations pick the same cells.

Where those cells are

The 162 calibration cells; outlined cells hold coretop data, filled cells are the analogues. Grey is land, drawn from the 1° climatology's coverage.

The τ² pairing defect (BTA-05), with 3 cells and 4 draws

MATLAB flattens α and β with a column-major reshape (cell changes fastest) but tiles τ² with repmat (draw changes fastest). Each column below is one flat position. Where the τ² draw differs from the α, β draw (outlined), the triple no longer comes from one posterior sample.

EarthChordDistances_2

EarthChordDistances_2.mbaysparpy/distance.pybayspar/observations/core.py · chord_distance

The straight-line distance between two points on a sphere of radius 6,378.137 km, through the Earth rather than along its surface. It decides which modern temperatures count as within 500 km.

The math

Haversine half-angle, then the chord

Half the central anglea = arcsin √( sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ cos φ₂ sin²(Δλ/2) )
Chordc = 2R sin a
For comparison, the arcs = 2R a

Chord against arc

Drag to change the separation. At the 500 km search radius the two differ by about 13 metres, so the choice never changes which cells are averaged in practice.

Which percentile

Three definitions of the 5th percentile

Percentiles of the numbers 1 to 10

MATLAB's prctile places sample i at (i − 0.5)/n and interpolates (Hazen). NumPy's default places it at (i − 1)/(n − 1). brews/baysparpy uses 'nearest', which snaps to a sample. Move the slider to compare.
